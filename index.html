Theoretical Foundations: Modern Psychological Perspectives II

Exploring Humanistic, Sociocultural, and Evolutionary perspectives; analyzing personality, personal values, social influence, cultural identity, needs hierarchy, motivation, ethical marketing, and stereotypes.

5-Min Retrieval Practice Warm-Up

Recall from Guide 6 that biological, behavioral, cognitive, and psychodynamic perspectives examine internal and unconscious mechanics. In Guide 7, we expand outward to examine how human potential (Humanistic), social and cultural contexts (Sociocultural), and ancestral survival strategies (Evolutionary) shape consumer identity, values, and brand choices.

1. Humanistic Perspective

Focuses on personal growth, self-actualization, free will, authentic values, and intrinsic motivation. Brands succeed when they empower customer self-fulfillment.

Key Concept: Self-Actualization & Values

2. Sociocultural Perspective

Emphasizes social influence, cultural norms, peer groups, identity, and belonging. Consumer preferences are deeply shaped by cultural symbols and social status expectations.

Key Concept: Culture, Norms & Belonging

3. Evolutionary Perspective

Examines how ancestral survival and reproductive drives manifest in modern consumer behavior, such as status signaling, risk avoidance, resource hoarding, and kin protection.

Key Concept: Ancestral Survival & Signaling
